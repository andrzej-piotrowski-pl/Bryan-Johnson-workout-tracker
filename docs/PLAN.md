# The plan (v2, October 2026) — readable snapshot

> This is the plan the app is built on, as one document you can read on GitHub. The app is the live version: it personalises heart rates and weights for each user and tracks everything. Numbers here are for the profile the plan was first written for (male, 38, 188 cm, 75 kg); each level column shows the weights that level would suggest for him.

A six-day indoor training plan laid out like Bryan Johnson’s newest weekly template (October 2026): strength Monday, Wednesday and Friday, short intervals Tuesday, the Norwegian 4×4 Thursday and easy Zone 2 Saturday. Rebuilt for bodyweight, adjustable dumbbells, a pull-up bar and resistance bands, with a daily posture block for tech neck and forward shoulders.

Made for: male, 38, 188 cm, 75 kg, trained before, four years off, no injuries.

- All sessions at home; Sunday rest
- Monday–Saturday: 34–45 min per session at New to training, 41–50 min at Returning, 53–60 min at Active, 54–66 min at Well trained
- Plus a posture block every day: ≈8 min, ≈10 min on Wednesday and Saturday
- Estimated max heart rate 182 bpm (Zone 2 = 109–127 bpm)

Each table lists the dose for each training level. A dash means the exercise is skipped at that level. Click an exercise name for how-to steps, the minute where Bryan does it, and demo videos.

## Training levels

Your training level alone decides the workouts; nothing moves on by itself. Change it in the app's Settings when you feel ready for harder exercises or want easier ones. Days you already logged keep the level they were done at.

### New to training

_Little or no strength training before._

**Effort 4–5 out of 10: stop each set with 5 or more easy reps left.**

- 1–2 sets, with supported or smaller-range versions (a hand on a chair or wall). Learn the positions first.
- No jumps, swings, power skips or Nordics yet; hops stay tiny.
- Tuesday: 4 brisk 20 s bursts. Thursday: 2 rounds of 2 min brisk, well below all-out.
- Zone 2 15–20 min. Mild muscle soreness is normal; joint pain is not.

_Move up to Returning after about 3 weeks (around 18 sessions) when every session feels comfortable and nothing hurts the next day._

### Returning

_Trained before, but out of practice for months or years._

**Effort 5–6 out of 10: stop each set with about 4 good reps left.**

- 2 sets on main lifts, 1 set on Bryan’s high-rep accessories. Relearn the positions.
- Jumps are landing drills only (snap-downs). Nordics are slow lowers only.
- Tuesday: 20 s brisk bursts with long recoveries. Thursday: 2 rounds of 3 min, then 3, controlled.
- Zone 2 20–25 min. Some muscle soreness is expected; joint pain is not. Still very sore after 48 hours? Repeat sessions before moving up.

_Move up to Active after about 2 weeks (around 12 sessions) when you reach the top of every rep range with clean form and soreness is gone within a day._

### Active

_Training regularly over the last few months._

**Effort about 7 out of 10: 2–3 reps left in each set.**

- 3 sets on most strength work. Real jumps start; stick every landing.
- Tuesday: 8 × 20 s hard. Thursday: 3 rounds of 4 min, then 4.
- Zone 2 25–35 min, plus short blocks on Monday.

_Move up to Well trained after about 4 weeks (around 24 sessions) when 4 rounds of the 4×4 stay controlled and your weights keep going up._

### Well trained

_Lifting and doing hard cardio consistently for a year or more._

**Effort 8 out of 10 on the main lifts (1–2 reps left); Bryan’s 1 × 25-style accessories go close to fatigue.**

- Galpin-style heavy work on the big lifts, with jump contrast after Friday’s RDL.
- Tuesday: two blocks of 8 × 20 s all-out. Thursday: the full Norwegian 4×4 at 155–173 bpm.
- Saturday Zone 2 grows to 40 min; a weighted vest is optional.

_This is the full plan. Keep adding weight with the rules below; after illness, injury or a long break, step down a level for a while._

### Rules

- Add weight only when you hit the top of the rep range on every set with clean form, two sessions in a row: about +1–2 kg per dumbbell for upper body, +2–4 kg for legs. Bryan’s protocol calls progressive overload the key, raised gradually.
- Injury prevention is Bryan’s priority #1. Swap to the easier variant (open the exercise) whenever a joint complains.
- The 4×4 is hard but controlled. If you can’t hold the pace in the last round, start slower next time.
- Rough week (bad sleep, stress, sickness): repeat sessions or step down a level for a while instead of pushing on.
- After four inactive years, get a basic check-up (blood pressure, resting ECG) before your first all-out intervals.

## Posture program

Built from Bryan’s posture guide (5 habits, updated March 2026) and the posture work in his routines, plus two classics you asked for (chin tucks and the doorway stretch), marked as not Bryan’s.

### Habits

1. **String cue.** Every time you sit down or stand up, imagine the string pulling the crown of your head up.
2. **Phone at eye level.** Bryan calls the phone the worst trigger for bad posture. Lift it to eye level instead of looking down; put the top of your monitor at eye level too.
3. **Move every 30 minutes.** Get up for 2–3 minutes every 30 minutes and rotate between sitting and standing, as Bryan does at his desk. Your under-desk treadmill is ideal for this.
4. **10 squats every ~45 minutes.** Bryan’s April 2026 tip for long sitting days, from a study where it beat one 30-minute walk for blood sugar. He stressed that breaking up the sitting is what matters.
5. **Two strength exercises, twice a week.** Shoulders up and out + neck forehead resistance, 1 set to fatigue each, on Wednesday and Saturday.
6. **Expect soreness.** Bryan’s guide: holding good posture takes muscle, and feeling sore at first is normal. Keep going. Sharp pain, tingling or headaches are different: stop and get it checked.

### How you’ll know you’re OK

- Every 2 weeks, take a relaxed side photo in the same spot. The ear should sit over the shoulder, not in front of it.
- Wall check: heels about 5 cm from a wall, buttocks and shoulder blades touching. The back of your head should reach the wall with your eyes level, without straining.
- Each evening, rate neck and upper-back stiffness from 0 to 10.
- When the photo and wall check look right and stiffness stays at 2 or below for 4 weeks, switch to maintenance: keep the two strength exercises twice a week and the habits (that is what Bryan keeps doing), and cut the daily block to 2–3 times a week.

### Warm-up & balance

#### Backward walk (sled substitute)

> **From Bryan:** Bryan opens his workouts with a backward sled drag (about 2 min in his 2023 routine, still first in his 2025 warm-up with Dr. Andy Galpin). It loads the knees and quads the Knees-Over-Toes way.

1. Walk backward for 2 min along your longest clear line indoors (hallway or living room), turning around at the wall.
2. Short steps; land on the ball of the foot and let the heel follow.
3. Keep the knees bent so they travel forward over the toes, and push the floor away in front of you.
4. From Returning up: hold a dumbbell in each hand, or loop a band around your waist anchored in front of you so it pulls you forward like a sled.

- Tip: It should burn in the front of the thighs, never hurt inside the knee.

#### Single-leg balance

> **From Bryan:** Balance is one of Bryan’s five exercise pillars (his May 2026 video). His free biological-age check is a one-leg stand with eyes closed; being able to stand 10 s on one leg is linked with lower all-cause mortality.

1. Stand on one leg next to a wall, hands on hips, other foot off the floor.
2. Hold 30 s, then progress to eyes closed.
3. Stop the clock when the foot shifts, a hand leaves the hip or the raised foot touches down.
4. Once a month, record your best eyes-closed time per leg.

### Power

#### Jumps: snap-down → squat jump → countermovement jump

> **From Bryan:** Since July 2026 Bryan trains to dunk at 49: heavy lifts with jumps between sets, aimed at the fast-twitch fibres and force development he says he lacks. He also flags Achilles risk from landing forces, so you learn to land before you jump high.

1. Snap-down (Returning): rise onto your toes with arms up, then drop fast into a quarter squat, arms back. Freeze 2 s.

#### Norwegian 4×4

> **From Bryan:** Thursday in Bryan’s newest weekly template (exercise newsletter, 4 Oct 2026): 4 rounds of 4 min at 85–95% of max heart rate with 3 min at 60–70% between, hard but controlled rather than an all-out sprint, starting with 2–3 rounds if four is too much. Bryan’s own variant (X, Dec 2024) is 4 min all-out with 4 min rest.

1. 10 min easy warm-up that builds to about 130 bpm.
2. 4 min hard: reach 155–173 bpm and hold an even pace you could repeat four times.
3. 3 min easy: march or step slowly (109–127 bpm). Keep moving.
4. Repeat, then 5 min easy. Indoors, change the move every minute so the legs don’t fail before the heart and lungs.

- Tip: If round 4 falls apart, start slower next time. Don’t sprint the first minute.

Watch: [4×4 HIIT with no equipment](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZOU0eUMYB8o) · [4×4 timing and heart rates explained](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EirgwA1FXUs)

#### Indoor interval moves

> **From Bryan:** Bryan’s published options for bodyweight HIIT: jumping jacks, high knees, mountain climbers, burpees without the push-up, jump squats, plus marching and bodyweight squats for beginners.

1. Fast step-ups on a stair or step: quietest and easiest to keep for 4 minutes.
2. High knees on the spot, driving the arms.
3. Mountain climbers, hips level.
4. Jumping jacks, or side-step jacks for a quiet version.
5. Burpees without push-up (step back and forward for a quiet version).

- Tip: For the 4×4, rotate step-ups → high knees → jacks → climbers, one minute each.

Watch: [Mountain climbers: 3 steps to proper form](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gW-q50V6Z_0) · [No-push-up burpee](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CqJ947Bj2Zg) · [Silent HIIT cardio (quiet move ideas)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gi7pZ8CYzvk)

#### Zone 2 cardio (indoors)

> **From Bryan:** Bryan’s protocol: at least 150 min a week of moderate activity (Zone 2) during which you can hold a conversation.

1. Heart rate 109–127 bpm (60–70% of max). You can speak in full sentences.
2. Main indoor option: continuous step-ups on a stable low aerobic step, switching the lead leg every minute. March if stepping raises your effort too much.
3. Mix in brisk marching with arm swings, side steps or easy jump rope to break the boredom.
4. Music, a podcast or a series helps; Bryan rotates music, books and podcasts too.

- Tip: When you get the chance, a brisk walk, hike or bike ride outside counts the same.

Watch: [Simple beginner step cardio](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aAHCk7dbnKM) · [10-minute low-impact step workout](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A0dUuUTe4jQ)

### Posture

#### String reset

> **From Bryan:** Habit 1 of Bryan’s posture guide: imagine a string going up through your spine and head, pulled straight up.

1. Stand or sit with the feet grounded.
2. Imagine the string lifting the crown of your head: chin level, neck long.
3. Let the shoulders settle down and back without forcing them.
4. 3 slow breaths. Repeat every time you sit down at the desk.

Watch: [Bryan: How I Fixed My Terrible Posture – 5 Habits at ≈1:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=in9ubCilsT8&t=104s) · [Bryan: A 20-Minute Call with My Posture Coach at ≈8:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cYGlj8Ryij8&t=513s)

#### Chin tucks

> **Not Bryan’s (classic):** Not one of Bryan’s exercises. A standard physiotherapy drill for forward head posture, added at your request.

1. Sit or stand tall, eyes level.
2. Glide your head straight back, making a slight double chin. Don’t nod down.
3. Hold 3 s, release slowly.

- Tip: Back against a wall makes it easier to feel the right direction.

Watch: [Physical therapist: how to do chin tucks](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ekJzJTnd43s) · [Chin tuck for neck pain and posture](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qtcBiYF7yuQ)

#### Wall slides

> **From Bryan:** In the dynamic stretching of Bryan’s official mobility day. Trains the shoulder blades to move up and back instead of forward.

1. Head, upper back and buttocks on a wall, feet a small step out.
2. Forearms on the wall in a W shape.
3. Slide the arms up into a Y keeping contact, then pull the elbows down toward the ribs.
4. Slow reps; ribs stay down.

Watch: [Scapular wall slides](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BiREOSisYWU) · [Wall slides for thoracic mobility and posture](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ti_FUZZz_7k)

#### Band pull-apart

> **From Bryan:** Bryan: 1 × 15.

1. Hold a light band at shoulder height, arms straight, hands shoulder-width.
2. Pull it apart to your chest by squeezing the shoulder blades together.
3. Shoulders stay down, away from the ears; pause 1 s.

Watch: [Bryan: My Complete Anti-Aging Workout (Jul 2023, gym version) at ≈4:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sYyVi-H-ozI&t=278s) · [Band pull-apart tutorial](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Zxp9IORjjK4) · [How to do band pull-aparts](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=smSSXITNpCI)

#### Band face pull

> **From Bryan:** Bryan: 1 × 15–25. His notes on this one stress good posture and a full range of motion.

1. Anchor the band at face height on the hinge side of a closed door.
2. Pull toward your eyes with elbows high and hands splitting apart.
3. Finish with thumbs pointing back and shoulder blades squeezed; hold 1 s.
4. Slow return; stay tall.

Watch: [Bryan: My Complete Anti-Aging Workout (Jul 2023, gym version) at ≈3:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sYyVi-H-ozI&t=217s) · [Band face pull with a door anchor](https://www.youtube.com/shorts/Q77ORURfeYc) · [Face pulls with a door anchor](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Fe37Ll4VDfI)

#### Shoulder external rotation

> **From Bryan:** “External rotation” is in the posture part of Bryan’s routine, right after band pull-aparts. It strengthens the rotator cuff that holds the shoulders back.

1. Sit with one foot up on a chair or bench and rest the elbow of the working arm on the inside of that knee, elbow bent 90°; or lie on your side with the elbow pinned to the ribs.
2. Hold a light dumbbell (1–4 kg) and rotate the forearm up and back as far as is comfortable.
3. Lower slowly in 2–3 s. Keep the shoulder blade back and down.

Watch: [Bryan: My Complete Anti-Aging Workout (Jul 2023, gym version) at ≈5:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sYyVi-H-ozI&t=302s) · [Dumbbell external rotation with the elbow on the knee](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7hfjcXtXi0w) · [Side-lying dumbbell external rotation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3XLT7kpMzcM)

#### Neck: forehead resistance

> **From Bryan:** Bryan’s physical-therapist exercise #2. Official wording (2026): “With a hand on your forehead, resist the pressure of your hand pushing your head back. When doing this, be very patient.” He does it to fatigue twice a week.

1. Sit or stand tall (string cue), chin slightly tucked so the back of the neck is long.
2. Put a hand on your forehead and push gently backward.
3. Resist with your neck. Either hold still, or let the hand win slowly over 3–5 s, moving the head back up to about 30°, then return.
4. Daily: 5 × 10 s holds at 30–50% effort. Wednesday and Saturday: 1 set to fatigue.

- Tip: Bryan calls this technical and was taught it for his own diagnosed condition. Stay gentle, and stop if you get dizziness, tingling or a headache.
- Tip: His 2023 version: neck long, chin down, light hand pressure on top of the head, lift the chin about 30° and lower, until fatigue.

Watch: [Bryan: How I Fixed My Terrible Posture – 5 Habits at ≈3:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=in9ubCilsT8&t=212s) · [Bryan: My Complete Anti-Aging Workout (Jul 2023, gym version) at ≈2:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sYyVi-H-ozI&t=165s) · [Bryan: A 20-Minute Call with My Posture Coach at ≈10:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cYGlj8Ryij8&t=634s)

#### Shoulders up and out (levator shrug)

> **From Bryan:** Bryan’s physical-therapist exercise #1. Official wording (2026): “With weights in your hands, bring your shoulders up and out. Keep your elbows back. Do 1 set to fatigue 2 times a week.” He uses about 9 kg (20 lb); his posture coach’s patients usually start near 3.5 kg (8 lb).

1. Stand tall with a light dumbbell in each hand, neck long, shoulders set back and down first.
2. Bend the elbows slightly and keep them pulled back, slightly behind the body.
3. Lift the shoulders up and out in a small arc, toward the ears and slightly forward.
4. Pause 1 s at the top, lower slowly. Learn it light: Bryan says it took him time to get the technique.

Watch: [Bryan: How I Fixed My Terrible Posture – 5 Habits at ≈2:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=in9ubCilsT8&t=158s) · [Bryan: My Complete Anti-Aging Workout (Jul 2023, gym version) at ≈2:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sYyVi-H-ozI&t=140s) · [Bryan: A 20-Minute Call with My Posture Coach at ≈11:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cYGlj8Ryij8&t=703s)

#### Doorway chest stretch

> **Not Bryan’s (classic):** Not one of Bryan’s exercises. A standard stretch for tight chest muscles that pull the shoulders forward, added at your request.

1. Forearms on the door frame, elbows at shoulder height and bent 90°.
2. Step one foot through and lean gently until you feel the stretch across the chest.
3. Ribs down, no low-back arch, no pinching in the shoulder.

Watch: [Physical therapy tip: doorway pec stretch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=069o76BafTk) · [Doorway pec and shoulder stretch](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Dmm8_S23I74)
